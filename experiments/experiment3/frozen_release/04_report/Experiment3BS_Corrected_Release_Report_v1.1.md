but did not ship code that produced them. In v1.1, `aggregate_results_v1_1.py` derives both files directly from `all_window_endpoints.csv`. The main runner invokes that aggregation automatically and records an additive-identity validation in `derived_table_validation.json`.

-
\Delta\text{pair part}
-

\]

across the three zeta datasets is below the fixed numerical tolerance.

## 3.2 The circular reconstruction check is retired

In the production implementation, the all-distinct term `Q4` is recovered as the residual after computing the one-, two-, and three-distinct-index classes. Adding those terms back necessarily closes the identity. That closure residual is therefore bookkeeping, not independent validation.

Release v1.1:

- renames it `algebraic_closure_residual_by_construction`;
- removes it from the integrity claims;
- states explicitly that production `Q4` is residual-based;
- adds direct ordered-quadruple enumeration that computes `Q4` independently.

The independent validator tested **54 deterministic cases** with matrix sizes 5, 6, and 7 and several core choices. It separately checked

\[
S_2,
\quad S_4,
\quad T_3,
\quad W_3^{(0)},
\quad W_3^{(1)},
\quad Q_4,
\]

as well as the one-, two-, three-, and four-distinct-index class totals. The largest term error was approximately

\[
3.69\times10^{-13},
\]

below the fixed tolerance `2e-12`.

The original package shipped a log but not the test source. Release v1.1 ships eight tests covering:

## 3.4 Both uncertainty scales are reported

Two distinct descriptive scales are now recorded:

1. **Individual-window scale:** divide the zeta--CUE mean difference by the standard deviation across individual CUE windows.
2. **16-window-mean scale:** divide by the standard deviation of 20,000 bootstrap means, each formed from 16 CUE windows.

The first answers how large a difference is relative to one window's variability. The second is the more relevant descriptive calibration for a 16-window dataset mean. Neither is promoted to a confirmatory probability statement, because the deterministic zeta windows are not asserted to be fully independent and the bounded protocol was exploratory.

The complete comparison is in `uncertainty_scale_comparison.csv`.

\qquad
B=\tfrac12\{0,1,6,7,9,11\},

The production result agrees with this value at floating-point precision.

This remains the cleanest conceptual result of Experiment 3BS: the fourth sinc-kernel moment can detect arrangement information that the unordered pair-distance multiset does not determine.

\[

\qquad

They correspond to about `-1.34`, `-1.40`, and `-1.17` individual-window CUE standard deviations, but about `-5.39`, `-5.62`, and `-4.72` bootstrap standard deviations of a 16-window CUE mean. The descriptive two-sided tails reached the finite resolution `1/(20000+1)`.

### D1 — near zero number \(10^{12}\)

D1 lies above CUE on all four displayed moment means. On the 16-window-mean bootstrap scale, the differences are approximately:

| Moment | Mean-scale difference |
|---|---:|
| \(\mu_2\) | +1.94 SD |
| \(\mu_3\) | +1.82 SD |
| raw \(\mu_4\) | +1.77 SD |
| \(\mu_4^{(>2)}\) | +1.07 SD |

The corresponding descriptive tails are approximately `0.053`, `0.067`, `0.079`, and `0.286`. This is a consistently signed but unresolved intermediate-height displacement, not a significant or confirmatory result.

### D2 — near zero number \(10^{21}\)

For `mu2`, raw `mu4`, and `mu4_gt2`, the mean-scale differences are approximately `+0.92`, `+0.51`, and `-0.37` bootstrap SD, with descriptive tails about `0.36`, `0.61`, and `0.71`.

D2 is therefore fully consistent with finite CUE at the resolution of the bounded study.

# 6. Corrected interpretation

Experiment 3BS supports the following statement:

> **The fourth normalized sinc-kernel moment is demonstrably capable of detecting arrangement information not fixed by an unordered pair-distance multiset. In the bounded zeta comparison, the first zeros are clearly displaced from finite CUE, the sample near zero number `10^12` is marginally high across the moment hierarchy, and the sample near `10^21` is CUE-consistent at the study's resolution. The collision decomposition shows that most of the raw fourth-moment displacement in the first two regimes remains pair-driven.**

It does not support:

- a new higher-order law for zeta zeros;
- independence of the residual from the complete two-point process;
- completion of the deferred full Experiment 3;
- holdout confirmation;
- evidence for or against the Riemann Hypothesis;
- validation of the Alpöge--Furman or Lamzouri proofs.
